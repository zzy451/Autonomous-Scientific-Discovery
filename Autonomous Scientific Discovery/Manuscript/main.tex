% Abstract (Do not insert blank lines, i.e. \\)
\abstract{Agent for Science systems connect multiple actions toward scientific research goals through planning, tool use, feedback, decision-making, or collaboration. This review examines their roles across scientific workflows using a core library of 662 distinct works, with the latest broad collection round ending on 22 October 2026. An explicit scientific-object hierarchy organizes the literature across eleven domains, with non-exclusive assignments supported by concrete experiments, benchmark tasks, case studies, or reported results. The review combines a classification snapshot with qualitative synthesis of representative systems. Selected cases demonstrate delegation of candidate selection, calibration, observation scheduling, and revisions to explanatory models, while problem formulation and interpretation retain substantial researcher input. Feasible delegation depends on how available actions and observations support the intended scientific claim, rather than on disciplinary maturity or textual accessibility alone. Primary comparisons establish distinct gains in experiment selection, generated procedures, and tool-assisted investigation; their attribution depends on prior information, comparators, resources, and human contributions. Mathematical witnesses, controlled investigation of unexpected phenomena, and experimentally tested process rules reveal distinct routes from agent outputs to new knowledge. Adaptive acquisition can improve candidate selection without improving general prediction or identifying a unique mechanism. Scientific validity consequently depends on the information accessible through the selected actions, the measurement relation, and inference appropriate to the acquired data.}
