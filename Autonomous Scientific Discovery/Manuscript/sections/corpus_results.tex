\section{Core Corpus and Classification Results}
\label{sec:corpus}

The 29 October 2026 snapshot contains 663 distinct core works. Of these, 654 have one or more concrete scientific-object domain assignments and nine remain in a separate general-method bucket. The latter bucket is an administrative distinction, rather than a twelfth scientific domain. Classification refinement changes the organization of this corpus; it does not extend the 22 October literature-search cutoff.

Among the 654 domain-assigned works, 196 are assigned to multiple domains. Together, the 654 works produce 926 non-exclusive work--domain memberships. Table~\ref{tab:corpuscounts} separates these memberships from primary filing counts. Primary filing selects one supported domain for ordering and storage; it does not eliminate the other supported domains. Domain counts describe the coverage of this assembled corpus and are sensitive to collection routes and coding decisions. They should not be interpreted as estimates of the relative prevalence or maturity of agents across all scientific research.

At the primary filing level, 653 of the 654 domain-assigned works have a tertiary position with a compatible secondary parent. One materials paper remains unresolved because accessible first-hand material does not adequately distinguish its most specific object category. Across all 926 work--domain memberships, 757 have a tertiary position and 169 remain without one; 87 of those memberships still lack a resolved internal secondary mapping. These are different denominators: nearly complete primary filing is not complete fine-grained coding of every additional domain.

The current ledger stores one representative tertiary position per work and domain. A paper can contain multiple objects within the same domain, so this representation is a navigation aid rather than an exhaustive inventory of all experiments. Administrative labels in the general-method bucket are excluded from formal tertiary completion counts. Earlier filing inconsistencies were repaired by aligning secondary parents with retained tertiary decisions; scientifically ambiguous cross-domain placements were separately checked against actual tasks. Such data reconciliation is distinct from independently reproducing the reported scientific findings.

Representative system profiles are presented in Section~\ref{sec:landscape}, followed by the within-domain analyses in Section~\ref{sec:domains}. These comparisons use object--task evidence rather than assigning one behavioral profile to every work in a module.
